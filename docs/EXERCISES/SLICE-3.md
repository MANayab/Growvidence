# Slice 3 — Learning Journal and AI-use disclosure exercise

**Status:** Write-yourself exercise. This is a learning guide, not an implementation file.

## Goal
Practice recording a learning attempt so it distinguishes activity from demonstrated skill and describes AI assistance transparently.

## Try it yourself
1. Choose a learning task you attempted recently. Record its title and the local date you worked on it (date-only `YYYY-MM-DD`, not a timestamp).
2. In **What I learned**, write 2–4 sentences in your own words. Avoid copying a tutorial or AI output.
3. In **What blocked me**, describe the specific error, confusion, or unresolved question.
4. In **What I'll do next**, state one small, verifiable next action.
5. Rate your confidence from 1 to 5. Treat it as a self-report, not a competency score.
6. Record minutes spent if you can estimate them honestly.
7. Select one AI disclosure mode:
   - `unassisted`: no AI assistance for this entry.
   - `assisted`: AI materially contributed.
   - `partially_assisted`: both you and AI contributed meaningful parts.
   - `unknown`: you cannot confidently reconstruct what assistance occurred.
   - `not_recorded`: you have not supplied a disclosure yet (default).
8. If applicable, name the tool and briefly note what it generated. Never label work unassisted merely because you edited generated output.
9. Save, then filter the journal by date, track and AI-use mode. Compare the five summary categories; unknown and not recorded should remain distinct.
10. Review your entry after a day or two. Update what you learned or your next step if your understanding changed.

## Reflection questions
- What can I reproduce without notes or AI?
- Which statement or test demonstrates my understanding?
- What uncertainty remains?
- What will I verify next?

## Success criteria
- Date remains local date-only.
- Learning, blocker and next step are distinct and concrete.
- Confidence is not presented as mastery.
- AI disclosure is honest; unknown/not recorded are never treated as unassisted.
- One next action is verifiable (e.g. write a test, reproduce an error, explain a decision).
